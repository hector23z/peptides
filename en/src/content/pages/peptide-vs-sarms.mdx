# Peptides vs SARMs

Research peptides and SARMs are both used in laboratory research, but they differ in their structure, mechanism and applications. This page compares the two classes for research purposes.

## What Are Research Peptides?

Research peptides are short chains of amino acids that act as signaling molecules, hormones or enzyme substrates in biological systems. They are used to study specific biological pathways, receptor interactions and cellular processes.

Research peptides are supplied as lyophilized powders that are reconstituted before use. They are used across a wide range of experimental settings, from cell culture studies to animal models.

## What Are SARMs?

SARMs, or selective androgen receptor modulators, are a class of compounds studied for their potential to selectively activate androgen receptor signaling. They are used to investigate the mechanisms of androgen action in experimental models.

SARMs are supplied as research compounds for use in experimental protocols. They are studied for their receptor selectivity and their effects on growth, metabolism and cellular function.

## Key Differences

Research peptides and SARMs differ in several important ways:

- **Structure** — peptides are chains of amino acids, while SARMs are small-molecule compounds
- **Mechanism** — peptides often act through receptor signaling, while SARMs target the androgen receptor
- **Applications** — peptides are used across metabolic, regenerative and endocrine research, while SARMs are used primarily in androgen research
- **Presentation** — peptides are supplied as lyophilized powders, while SARMs are supplied as research compounds

## How They Are Used in Research

Research peptides are used to study a wide range of biological systems, including GLP-1 receptor signaling, tissue repair and cellular energy metabolism. SARMs are used to study androgen receptor signaling and receptor selectivity.

The choice between a peptide and a SARM depends on the research question. Investigators select the compound class that best matches the biological system and pathway they wish to study.

## Choosing Between Peptides and SARMs

The choice between a research peptide and a SARM depends on the research question. If the study involves incretin signaling, tissue repair or cellular metabolism, a peptide may be the appropriate tool. If the study involves androgen receptor signaling, a SARM may be more relevant.

Researchers should review the published literature for their research area to identify the most appropriate compound class and presentation for their protocol. Both classes require careful attention to quality and documentation.
