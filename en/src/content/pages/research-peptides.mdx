# Research Peptides

Research peptides are short chains of amino acids supplied for laboratory investigation. This guide explains what they are, the main classes used in research and how to work with them safely and responsibly.

## What Are Research Peptides?

Peptides are short chains of amino acids linked by peptide bonds. They are smaller than proteins and often act as signaling molecules, hormones or enzyme substrates in biological systems. In a research context, synthetic peptides are produced to study specific biological pathways, receptor interactions and cellular processes.

Research peptides are supplied as lyophilized (freeze-dried) powders that are reconstituted before use. They are used in a wide range of experimental settings, from cell culture studies to animal models, to investigate the mechanisms underlying physiological processes.

Because peptides are highly specific in their activity, they are valuable tools for isolating and studying individual biological pathways. A single peptide can be used to probe receptor function, examine downstream signaling or investigate the effects of a particular hormone in a controlled experimental system.

## Main Classes of Research Peptides

Research peptides are commonly grouped by the biological system they are used to study:

- **GLP-1 receptor agonists** — studied in metabolic and incretin research, including semaglutide, tirzepatide and retatrutide.
- **Recovery peptides** — studied in tissue repair and regenerative research, including BPC-157, TB-500 and GHK-Cu.
- **Longevity peptides** — studied in aging and cellular health research, including NAD+, epitalon and MOTS-c.
- **Growth hormone peptides** — studied in endocrine and growth research, including HGH, ipamorelin and CJC-1295.

Each class targets a distinct area of investigation, and researchers select compounds based on the specific questions they are asking. Understanding the classification of research peptides helps investigators choose the right material for their experimental design.

## How Research Peptides Are Used

In the laboratory, research peptides are used to study receptor activation, signaling pathways and physiological responses. Common applications include:

- Investigating receptor binding and downstream signaling
- Studying metabolic and endocrine regulation
- Examining tissue repair and regeneration processes
- Exploring cellular energy metabolism and aging-related pathways

Because peptides are supplied as lyophilized powders, they must be reconstituted with an appropriate solvent before use. The reconstitution protocol, storage conditions and administration route all depend on the specific study design and should follow published literature and institutional guidelines.

## Quality and Purity Considerations

The quality of research peptides is a critical consideration. High-purity material reduces the risk of confounding results and improves reproducibility. When sourcing research peptides, researchers should look for:

- Independently verified purity, typically determined by high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC)
- A certificate of analysis (CoA) documenting test results
- Clear documentation of peptide content and molecular weight
- Reliable batch tracking and consistent quality control

Purity is especially important in studies where impurities could interfere with receptor binding or downstream measurements. A certificate of analysis provides the documentation needed to confirm that the material meets its stated specifications.

## Handling and Storage

Proper handling and storage are essential for maintaining peptide quality. Lyophilized powders should be stored in a cool, dry place, protected from light and moisture. Reconstituted material should be refrigerated and used within the timeframe specified by the research protocol. Avoiding repeated freeze-thaw cycles helps preserve peptide stability.

## Research Use Only

Research peptides are intended for laboratory research purposes only. They are not for human or veterinary consumption, and they are not approved by any regulatory authority for medical use. Researchers should handle all peptides in accordance with their institutional biosafety and research guidelines.
