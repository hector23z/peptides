# L-Carnitine

L-carnitine is a quaternary ammonium compound studied in laboratory research for its role in cellular energy metabolism. It is supplied as a research compound for use in experimental protocols.

## What Is L-Carnitine?

L-carnitine is a naturally occurring compound that plays a central role in the transport of long-chain fatty acids into the mitochondria, where they are oxidized for energy production. It is studied in research on cellular energy metabolism and related physiological processes.

In laboratory research, L-carnitine is used to investigate fatty acid metabolism, mitochondrial function and energy balance. It is supplied as a research compound for use in experimental protocols.

## Role in Fatty Acid Metabolism

L-carnitine is essential for the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane. This transport is a key step in the beta-oxidation of fatty acids, which is the process by which fatty acids are broken down to produce energy.

In research, L-carnitine is studied for its role in:

- Fatty acid transport into the mitochondria
- Beta-oxidation and energy production
- Mitochondrial function and metabolism
- Regulation of cellular energy balance
