# NAD+

NAD+ (nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide) is a coenzyme studied in laboratory research settings for its central role in cellular metabolism. It is supplied as a lyophilized powder for reconstitution in research protocols.

## What is NAD+?

NAD+ is a ubiquitous coenzyme found in all living cells, where it participates in redox reactions and serves as a substrate for several enzyme families. It is studied in the context of cellular energy metabolism, DNA repair and the regulation of enzymes involved in aging-related research.

The coenzyme is of interest in longevity research because of its role in supporting the activity of sirtuins and other NAD+-dependent enzymes. Researchers use NAD+ to investigate cellular energetics and metabolic regulation in experimental models.

NAD+ is supplied as a lyophilized powder that is stable when stored correctly, making it well suited to multi-day research protocols. Its documented stability and reproducible handling are among the reasons it is a popular choice for investigators working in cellular metabolism and aging-related research.

## How It Works (Research Context)

In laboratory models, NAD+ functions as a key electron carrier in cellular respiration, shuttling electrons between metabolic reactions. It also serves as a substrate for NAD+-dependent enzymes, including sirtuins and poly(ADP-ribose) polymerases, which are studied for their roles in cellular regulation.

Research applications focus on:

- Cellular energy metabolism and redox balance
- NAD+-dependent enzyme activity
- Sirtuin and DNA repair pathway research
- Metabolic regulation in experimental models
