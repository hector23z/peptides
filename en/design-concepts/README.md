# PeptideLab homepage concept study

All four concepts use the same functional brief: a high-fidelity, full-page desktop ecommerce homepage for research peptides, including a research-use notice, practical navigation, asymmetric hero, category directory and featured product catalogue. Every direction excludes people, medical claims, gradients, glow shadows, generic icon tiles, eyebrow labels and illegible contrast.

## Prompt set

- **A — Dark cyan:** Premium dark scientific laboratory, off-black and graphite surfaces, cool white text, a single cyan-teal accent, low-key vial photography with controlled cyan rim light, fine rules and an image-led ecommerce hierarchy.
- **B — Light clinical:** Warm-white pharmaceutical catalogue, pale cool-gray panels, deep ink text, a single teal accent, bright diffused laboratory photography and transparent Swiss-style information design.
- **C — Dark amber:** Warm near-black luxury scientific archive, bone text, antique-gold accent, museum-grade glass photography, slim brass rules and an editorial catalogue rhythm that stays sober rather than mystical.
- **D — Technical brutalist:** Off-white and carbon planes, rigid grid, oversized condensed typography, exposed record metadata, stark monochrome vial cutouts and a sparse signal-green accent.

Shared page copy: “High-purity compounds for controlled research”, “Browse compounds”, “Research use only”, “Featured compounds” and “Research categories”. Shared catalogue examples: BPC-157, Semaglutide, GHK-Cu and CJC-1295; Recovery, GLP-1 Research, Longevity and HGH Research.

## Evaluation

| Concept | Trust | Conversion | Differentiation | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A — Dark cyan | High | High | High | Selected |
| B — Light clinical | Very high | High | Low | Credible but category-generic |
| C — Dark amber | Medium | Medium | High | Luxury cues weaken scientific neutrality |
| D — Technical brutalist | High | Medium-low | Very high | Memorable but dense for catalogue browsing |

Concept A offers the strongest combined result. The production UI also adopts D's best trait — visible record metadata — while retaining A's calmer hierarchy and familiar ecommerce scanning patterns.
